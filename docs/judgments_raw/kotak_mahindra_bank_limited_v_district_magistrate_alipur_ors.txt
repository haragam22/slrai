                           &quot;18. It is necessary to refer to the settled law on the
                        scope of  Sections 34  of the Act. In this case we are
                        concerned with the point as to whether an arbitral
                        award can be set aside for being in conflict with the
                        public policy of India. An arbitral award can be set
                        aside if it is contrary to (a) fundamental policy of Indian
                        law, or (b) the interest of India, or (c) justice or
                        morality. ( Renusagar Power Co. Ltd. v. General
                        Electric Co. ,(1994) Supp.  1 SCC 644)                 Patent
                        illegality was added to the above three grounds in
                        ONGC v. Saw Pipes Ltd. (2003) 5 SCC 705. Illegality
                        must go to the root of the matter and incase the
                        illegality is of trivial nature it cannot be held that the
                        award is against the public policy. It was further
                        observed in the said judgment (ONGC v. Saw Pipes
                        (supra)) that an award could also be set aside if it is
                        so unfair and unreasonable that it shocks the
                        conscience of the Court. In Delhi Development
                        Authority v. M/s. R.S. Sharma &amp; Co., (2008) 13 SCC
                        80 it was held that an award can be interfered with by

                        the Court under  Section 34  of the Act when it is
                        contrary to :
                          a) substantive provisions of law; or
                          b) provisions of the 1996 Act; or
                          c) against the terms of the respective contract; or
                          d) patently illegal; or
                          e) prejudicial to the rights of the parties.
                                 The fundamental policy of India was explained in
                        ONGC Ltd. v. Western Geco International Co. Ltd.
                        (2014) 9 SCC 263 as including all such fundamental
                        principles as providing a basis for administration of
                        justice and enforcement of law in this country. It was
                        held inter alia, that a duty is cast on every tribunal or
                        authority exercising powers that affect the rights or
                        obligations of the parties to show a &#x27;judicial approach&#x27;.

                        an authority acts bona fide and deals with the subject
                        in a fair, reasonable and objective manner and its
                        decision is not actuated by any extraneous
                        considerations. It was also held that the requirement of
                        application of mind on the part of the adjudicatory
                        authority is so deeply embedded in our jurisprudence
                        that it can be described as a fundamental policy of
                        Indian law. This Court further observed that the award
                        of the Arbitral Tribunal is open to challenge when the
                        arbitrators fail to draw an inference which ought to be
                        drawn or if they had drawn an inference which on the
                        face of it is untenable resulting in miscarriage of

                        offending part of the award in case it is severable from
                        the rest according to the said judgment (Western Geco
                        ltd. (supra)).
                           19. The limit of exercise of power by Courts under

                          a) The Court under  Section 34(2)  of the Act, does not

                        &quot;public policy&quot; to an arbitral award and consequently

                          b) A possible view by the arbitrator on facts has

                          c) Insufficiency of evidence cannot be a ground for
                        interference by the Court. Re­examination of the facts

         said period of limitation would commence from the date of the last
         communication between the parties, therefore, claim of the
         respondent cannot be stated to be barred by limitation.
 

     13.                 As far as the contention of the petitioner that the ld.
           Arbitrator has passed the impugned award after the period of one
         year and therefore, the impugned award deserves to be set aside
         on this ground alone is concerned, the said contention appears to
         be attractive but the same is fallacious in as much as  Section 29A 
         was inserted, by way of amendments to the  Indian Arbitration and
         Conciliation Act  in the year 2015 and with the introduction of this
         provision, the time­period for passing the award has been fixed at
         twelve months from the date the arbitral tribunal enters upon
         reference and is extendable by another six months with the
         consent of the parties. Any further extensions can only be granted
         by the concerned court, either prior to or after the expiry of the time
         period, failing which the mandate of the arbitral tribunal shall
         terminate.
 

     14.                 It may be noted that the ld. Arbitrator on 05.09.2017 i.e.
         from the one year of his entering into the reference, has extended the
         period of passing of the award for another six months with the
         consent of the parties which fact has been so recorded in the
         ordersheet dated 05.09.2017. In view of the same, this objection of
         the petitioner is also deserves to be rejected and is rejected.

      15.                 Now coming to the last objection that the arbitral
         proceedings commenced at Delhi is beyond the terms and
         conditions of the Arbitration clause in the loan agreement.  In this
         regard, it may be noted that counsel for the petitioner by placing
         reliance upon  Bhandari Udyog Ltd. v. Industrial Facilitation
         Council &amp; Anr.  AIR 2015 SCC 1320 has argued that the courts at
         Gurgaon was having the jurisdiction to entertain any dispute
         between the parties in respect of the loan agreement, however, the
         arbitral proceedings have been initiated at Delhi.   It may be noted
         that the ratio of  Bhandari Udyog  (supra) is to the effect that when
         arbitral proceedings has been conducted within the jurisdiction of
         Raichur Court, which is subordinate to the High Court of
         Karnataka, the award cannot be challenged before the court
         subordinate to High Court of Bombay. However, in the present
         case, the arbitration clause as reproduced above provide for that
         the arbitration proceedings will be held at New Delhi and any other
         dispute arising of the said matter shall be subjected to the
         exclusive jurisdiction of the courts at Gurgaon, Haryana. A bare
         perusal of the Clause 29 depicts that the venue of Arbitration will
         be in Delhi therefore, naturally the proceedings for challenging the
         Award will lie before the courts at Delhi.   Therefore, the facts of the
         case law  Bhandari Udyog  (supra) is not applicable to the fact of
         the present case.       In view of the same, this objection of the
         petitioner is also deserves to be rejected and is rejected. 
 

      16.                 From the above discussion, it can safely concluded
         that none of the grounds as agitated in this petition is falling within

 Arbtn. No.: 1339/2018         Dara Singh v. M/s Kotak Mahindra Bank &amp; Ors.       DOD : 25.02.2021


         the four corners of the definition of  Section 34  of Arbitration &amp;
         Conciliation Act, so as to empower this court to interfere with the
         finding recorded in the impugned award, otherwise, also, these
         findings of facts have been recorded on the basis of facts and the
         material on record and such finding of fact are not supposed to be
         interfered as per the dicta of law  laid down by  superior courts. 
 

     17.                 In view of the above said reasons, the petition under
          Section 34  of Arbitration &amp; Conciliation Act 1996, filed by the
         petitioner is dismissed and disposed off accordingly.
