## BORROWER'S CLAIM

The borrowers alleged that no Section 13(2) or Section 13(4) notice was served upon them in compliance with the SARFAESI Rules, 2002, and that they first became aware of the enforcement proceedings only on 15.04.2025 when a notice under Section 14 was affixed on their property. They contended that the cause of action for filing a Securitisation Application (SA) under Section 17 arose from the date of the Section 14 order, not from the earlier possession notice allegedly served in 2023. They further argued that the DRT erred in dismissing their SA as barred by limitation while simultaneously adjudicating the merits of the Section 14 order, thereby violating principles of natural justice. The prayer before the High Court was to set aside the DRT’s order and remand the matter for fresh adjudication on merits with interim protection.

## HOLDING SUMMARY

Section 17 of the SARFAESI Act provides a continuous cause of action to the borrower to challenge any measure taken under Section 13(4), including the issuance of a possession notice under Rule 8(1) and Rule 8(2), and subsequent actions under Section 14. The limitation period for filing an SA under Section 17 begins from the date of the last actionable step challenged, not from the earliest notice. A Section 14 order by the CMM constitutes a further enforcement action under Section 13(4) and gives rise to a fresh cause of action. The DRT cannot dismiss an SA as time-barred while simultaneously reviewing the validity of the Section 14 order on merits. When a borrower challenges a Section 14 order and the DRT dismisses the SA on limitation grounds without considering the continuous nature of actionable steps under Section 13(4), the order is legally unsustainable. This applies when: the borrower files an SA after a Section 14 order and the DRT dismisses it as time-barred, ignoring the continuous cause of action under Section 17.

## KEY FACTS OF THIS CASE

The petitioners had availed a credit facility from a bank, which was later assigned to respondent no.3. The loan account became NPA, and enforcement proceedings were initiated under SARFAESI. The bank issued a possession notice under Section 13(4) dated 18.05.2023, which the DRT found duly served. On 10.03.2025, the Additional District Magistrate (ADM) passed an order under Section 14 permitting physical possession. This order was affixed on the property on 15.04.2025, which is when the petitioners first learned of the proceedings. They filed S.A. No.405 of 2025 before the DRT on 21.04.2025, challenging the enforcement and seeking interim relief, along with a delay condonation application. The DRT dismissed the SA as time-barred from 18.05.2023 and rejected interim relief. The petitioners challenged this in the High Court under Article 227.

## WHAT THE COURT DECIDED

The High Court set aside the DRT’s order dated 17.06.2025 dismissing the SA as time-barred and remanded the matter for fresh adjudication on merits. It held that the DRT erred in treating the SA as hopelessly barred by limitation while simultaneously reviewing the validity of the Section 14 order. The Court directed the DRT to decide the interim relief application afresh and imposed a status quo on the property until further orders. It also directed the DRT to decide the interim application within one month of receiving the certified copy of the judgment.

## KEY QUOTE

Ex-facie, the observations made by the DRT that the S.A. was hopelessly barred by limitation is utterly erroneous as the starting point of limitation has been considered by the DRT to be the service of notice under Section 13(4) and not the knowledge derived by the petitioners as pleaded by them, from the date when the order under Section 14 was affixed.

## CONDITION: WHEN THIS JUDGMENT APPLIES

This judgment applies when the borrower challenges a Section 14 order and the DRT dismisses the SA as barred by limitation, ignoring continuous cause of action under Section 17.

## CONDITION: WHEN THIS JUDGMENT DOES NOT APPLY

1. When the borrower challenges only the initial demand notice under Section 13(2) and not any subsequent action — in that case, *Kanaiyalal Lalchand Sachdev v. State of Maharashtra* applies, and limitation starts from the 13(2) notice.
2. When the SA is filed after an auction sale and challenges the sale certificate — in that case, *Celir LLP v. Bafna Motors* applies, and limitation is strictly interpreted.
3. When the borrower had actual knowledge of the 13(4) notice before the Section 14 order — in that case, limitation starts from the 13(4) notice, and this judgment does not apply.

## RELATIONSHIP TO OTHER JUDGMENTS

Follows: Mardia Chemicals Ltd. v. Union of India (2004) 4 SCC 311
  Established that Section 17 provides a remedy against any measure under Section 13(4), and proceedings are in the nature of original jurisdiction, not appeal.

Follows: Hindon Forge Private Limited v. State of Uttar Pradesh (2019) 2 SCC 198
  Clarified that possession notice under Rule 8(1) and Rule 8(2) triggers Section 17 jurisdiction and constitutes a measure under Section 13(4).

Distinguishes: Kanaiyalal Lalchand Sachdev v. State of Maharashtra (2011) 2 SCC 782
  Kanaiyalal held that Section 14 is post-13(4), but did not address the continuous cause of action. This case distinguishes it by holding that Section 14 gives a fresh cause of action.

Affirmed: M/s. Alpine Pharmaceuticals Pvt. Ltd. (Supra)
  Affirmed the principle that each step under Section 13(4) gives a separate cause of action and limitation starts from the last such step.
