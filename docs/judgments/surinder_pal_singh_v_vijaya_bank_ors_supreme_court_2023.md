## BORROWER'S CLAIM

This judgment does not involve a SARFAESI Act enforcement or a borrower's challenge to a bank's recovery action. It is a criminal appeal concerning the conviction of two appellants for murder and destruction of evidence under Sections 302 and 201 read with Section 34 of the Indian Penal Code. There is no borrower's claim related to a secured asset, demand notice, possession, or auction.

## HOLDING SUMMARY

This judgment is not related to the SARFAESI Act, 2002, or the enforcement of security interests by banks. It is a criminal appeal decided by the High Court of Himachal Pradesh, which upheld the conviction of the appellants for the offences of murder under Section 302 IPC and causing disappearance of evidence under Section 201 IPC, both read with Section 34 IPC, based on circumstantial evidence. The court reaffirmed the principles for appreciating circumstantial evidence, including the necessity for the chain of circumstances to be complete, conclusive, and inconsistent with the innocence of the accused. The appeal was dismissed, and the conviction and sentence were upheld. This applies when: a criminal case involves conviction based on circumstantial evidence, including last seen theory, recovery of evidence, and motive, under the Indian Penal Code.

## KEY FACTS OF THIS CASE

This is a criminal case, not a SARFAESI matter. The facts are that the appellants, Karan Singh and Virender, were accused of murdering Karan's uncle, Prem Raj, allegedly due to a motive of molestation of Karan's wife. The deceased was last seen with the appellants, and his partially burnt body was found under a bridge. The appellants were arrested, and during investigation, they led the police to recover the deceased's personal belongings, a stone possibly used as a weapon, and the deceased's mobile phone and voter ID from their respective huts. The prosecution relied on circumstantial evidence, including call detail records, witness testimony, and forensic reports, to establish guilt. The trial court convicted them, and the High Court upheld the conviction.

## WHAT THE COURT DECIDED

The High Court dismissed the appeal and upheld the conviction of the appellants for the offences under Sections 302 and 201 IPC read with Section 34 IPC. The court confirmed the sentences of life imprisonment for murder and three years of rigorous imprisonment for destruction of evidence, to run concurrently. The court found that the chain of circumstantial evidence was complete and pointed unerringly towards the guilt of the appellants.

## KEY QUOTE

All these circumstances, taken together, point towards the guilt of the accused. No explanation was provided by the accused, which would be in consonance with their innocence.
