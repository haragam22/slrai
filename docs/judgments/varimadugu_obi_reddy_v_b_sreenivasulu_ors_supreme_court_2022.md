## BORROWER'S CLAIM

The borrowers alleged that the e-auction sale was vitiated due to two defects: first, a typographical error in the description of the mortgaged property in the auction notice, where the door number was incorrectly stated as "12-3-393" instead of "12-3-39", which allegedly caused the property to fetch a lower value. Second, they contended that the auction purchaser failed to deposit the balance 75% of the bid amount by the 15th day as required under Rule 9(4) of the SARFAESI Rules, 2002, having deposited it on the 15th April 2015 instead of the 11th April 2015, thereby breaching a mandatory provision. They argued that this breach rendered the sale void and entitled them to have all enforcement proceedings set aside.

## HOLDING SUMMARY

Rule 9(4) of the Security Interest (Enforcement) Rules, 2002, as it stood prior to amendment, allowed for extension of the 15-day period for depositing the balance 75% of the auction amount if there was a written agreement between the parties, and the term "parties" includes the secured creditor, borrower, and auction purchaser. However, a minor delay in payment not exceeding a few days, especially when caused by ongoing tribunal proceedings and at the bank's request, does not automatically trigger forfeiture under Rule 9(5) or invalidate the sale. A typographical error in the door number of the mortgaged property, where the full description including boundaries, measurements, and location remains accurate and no prejudice is demonstrated, does not vitiate the auction process. The enforcement proceedings cannot be nullified on such technical grounds when the auction was substantially compliant and the borrower failed to deposit required interim amounts. This applies when: the auction purchaser delayed balance payment by a few days due to tribunal proceedings and the bank requested the delay, and the property description error was merely typographical with no actual ambiguity.

## KEY FACTS OF THIS CASE

The borrowers availed three loans totaling Rs.26 lakhs from the bank, secured by a residential property in Anantapur. The account was classified as NPA on 30.09.2012. A demand notice under Section 13(2) was issued on 15.11.2012, followed by possession under Section 13(4) on 14.02.2013. The DRT dismissed the borrowers' first SA challenging possession in 2014. An e-auction was conducted on 28.03.2015 with a reserve price of Rs.55.33 lakhs. The appellant emerged as the highest bidder at Rs.64.23 lakhs, depositing 25% on 28.03.2015 and the balance 75% on 15.04.2015, four days after the 11.04.2015 deadline. The borrowers challenged the auction notice in a fresh SA, citing a door number error and the payment delay. The DRT dismissed the SA, but the Telangana High Court set aside the sale. The Supreme Court reversed the High Court’s decision.

## WHAT THE COURT DECIDED

The Supreme Court allowed the appeal, quashed the impugned judgment of the Telangana High Court, and upheld the e-auction sale. It held that the four-day delay in depositing the balance 75% of the bid amount did not vitiate the sale, especially given the ongoing tribunal proceedings and the bank’s role in requesting the delay. The typographical error in the door number was held to be inconsequential as the full property description was accurate and no prejudice was proven. The Court directed the bank to transfer the surplus amount of Rs.16.30 lakhs (with accrued interest, totaling approx. Rs.18.80 lakhs) to the borrowers’ or guarantors’ account within eight weeks.

## KEY QUOTE

A mere typographical error due to inadvertence which has not caused any prejudice to the borrowers, that in itself could not be considered to be the ground to annul the process held by the secured creditor.

## CONDITION: WHEN THIS JUDGMENT APPLIES

This judgment applies when: the auction purchaser delayed balance payment by a few days due to tribunal proceedings and the bank requested the delay, and the property description error was merely typographical with no actual ambiguity.

## CONDITION: WHEN THIS JUDGMENT DOES NOT APPLY

1. When the auction purchaser failed to deposit the balance 75% of the bid amount beyond 18 days from the auction date — in that case, Rule 9(5) forfeiture applies and the sale may be void.
2. When the property description error caused actual confusion or misled bidders, such as when another property exists with the incorrect door number — in that case, the auction may be vitiated for lack of transparency.
3. When the bank did not request the delay and the purchaser unilaterally defaulted — in that case, the delay is not excusable and Rule 9(5) applies.

## STATUTORY CONTEXT

Primary law: Security Interest (Enforcement) Rules, 2002  
Primary provision: Rule 9(4) — "The balance amount of purchase price payable shall be paid by the purchaser to the authorised officer on or before the fifteenth day of confirmation of sale of the immovable property or such extended period as may be agreed upon in writing between the parties."  
Secondary provision: Rule 9(5) — "In default of payment within the period mentioned in sub-rule (4), the deposit shall be forfeited and the property shall be resold..."  
Instrument level: RULES  
Nature of provision: DIRECTORY (pre-amendment) — court held the 15-day period is not sacrosanct and can be extended by conduct or agreement, especially when no prejudice is caused.
