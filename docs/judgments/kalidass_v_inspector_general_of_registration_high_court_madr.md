## BORROWER'S CLAIM

The petitioner, A. Elangovan, filed a writ petition under Article 226 of the Constitution of India seeking a writ of mandamus to direct the first respondent, the Deputy Inspector General of Registration, to dispose of his representation dated 10.05.2023 in accordance with law. The claim was that the authority had failed to act on his representation, causing delay and administrative inaction. The prayer was for the court to compel the authority to decide the representation.

## HOLDING SUMMARY

This case does not establish any legal principle under the SARFAESI Act or related enforcement rules, as it pertains to a writ petition under Article 226 concerning administrative inaction in the registration department. The petition was dismissed as withdrawn, with no adjudication on the merits of the claim. No precedent was set regarding secured asset enforcement, demand notices, auction procedures, or borrower rights. The court granted liberty to approach the civil court, indicating that the matter remains open for adjudication in an appropriate forum. This applies when: a writ petition challenging administrative delay is withdrawn before any substantive order is passed.

## KEY FACTS OF THIS CASE

The petitioner, A. Elangovan, had submitted a representation to the Deputy Inspector General of Registration on 10.05.2023, which remained unaddressed. Aggrieved by the inaction, he filed W.P.No.17211 of 2025 in the Madras High Court under Article 226 seeking mandamus. The matter was listed for hearing on 04.06.2025, at which point the petitioner’s counsel sought to withdraw the petition. The court permitted withdrawal and dismissed the petition without costs, granting liberty to approach the civil court.

## WHAT THE COURT DECIDED

The Madras High Court dismissed the writ petition as withdrawn, recording the submission of the petitioner’s counsel. The court did not adjudicate on the merits of the claim or issue any directions regarding the disposal of the representation. It granted liberty to the petitioner to approach the civil court for redressal of his grievance.

## KEY QUOTE

Recording the said submission made by learned counsel for petitioner, this Writ Petition is dismissed as withdrawn with liberty to approach the civil Court.

## CONDITION: WHEN THIS JUDGMENT APPLIES

Applies when a writ petition under Article 226 is withdrawn before adjudication on merits.

## CONDITION: WHEN THIS JUDGMENT DOES NOT APPLY

1. When the matter involves a SARFAESI enforcement action such as demand notice, possession, or auction — in such cases, judgments like Kanaiyalal or Celir LLP apply.
2. When the writ petition is decided on merits — then the holding of that decision applies, not this withdrawal.
3. When the petitioner does not withdraw but pursues relief — this judgment has no precedential value for contested matters.
