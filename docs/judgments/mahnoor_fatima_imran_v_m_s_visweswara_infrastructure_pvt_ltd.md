## BORROWER'S CLAIM

The borrowers (writ petitioners) alleged that they were in lawful possession of the disputed 53-acre land based on registered sale deeds issued by Bhavana Co-operative Housing Society Ltd., which had acquired the property under an agreement of sale dated 19.03.1982. They contended that their title, being registered, should be presumed valid and binding unless set aside by a competent civil court. They further claimed protection against dispossession by TSIIC without due process of law, relying on interim orders from the High Court in earlier proceedings. The prayer was to restrain the respondents from entering or demolishing structures on the land.

## HOLDING SUMMARY

A registered sale deed does not confer indefeasible title if it is based on a void or unenforceable transaction such as an unregistered agreement of sale or a GPA sale, especially when the vendor lacked valid title at the time of execution. The Supreme Court reaffirmed the principle from Suraj Lamp & Industries Pvt. Ltd. v. State of Haryana that transactions involving "SA/GPA/will" do not amount to legal transfer of immovable property and cannot be recognised as valid modes of conveyance. Such documents do not convey title and are not protected under Section 53-A of the Transfer of Property Act unless supported by a valid underlying transaction. When the vendor itself had no title—having lost it to the State under the Land Reforms Act—the chain of title fails ab initio. Mere registration of a deed does not cure this defect, and the presumption under Section 112 of the Evidence Act is rebuttable. This applies when: third-party purchasers claim title based on unregistered agreements and GPA, and the vendor lacked valid title at the time of sale.

## KEY FACTS OF THIS CASE

The original owners of 526.07 acres in Survey No.83, Raidurg, were declared surplus under the Andhra Pradesh Land Reforms (Ceiling on Agricultural Holdings) Act, 1973, and possession was taken by the State in 1975. Despite this, a GPA holder executed an unregistered agreement of sale dated 19.03.1982 in favour of Bhavana Society for 99.07 acres, which was later "revalidated" in 2006 without registration. Bhavana Society filed a suit for specific performance in 1991, which was dismissed for default in 2001. Subsequently, Bhavana Society executed sale deeds to various purchasers, including the writ petitioners, who claimed possession and title. The State, through TSIIC, sought to take possession for development. The writ petitioners challenged this, claiming protection under Article 226. The Single Judge dismissed the petition, finding title and possession suspect; the Division Bench reversed, relying on interim orders; the Supreme Court restored the Single Judge’s order.

## WHAT THE COURT DECIDED

The Supreme Court set aside the Division Bench judgment and restored the Single Judge’s order dismissing the writ petition. It held that the writ petitioners failed to establish a valid title or lawful possession, as their claim rested on an unregistered agreement of sale and GPA transactions, which do not convey title. The Court found the chain of title defective since the original vendor had no title after vesting in the State. The revalidation of the 1982 agreement in 2006 did not cure the legal infirmities. The interim orders relied upon did not establish actual possession. The petitioners were not entitled to protection under Article 226.

## KEY QUOTE

Transactions of the nature of 'GPA sales' or 'SA/GPA/will transfers' do not convey title and do not amount to transfer, nor can they be recognised or valid mode of transfer of immovable property.

## CONDITION: WHEN THIS JUDGMENT APPLIES

This judgment applies when:
1. `ats_date` is prior to statutory vesting — the agreement to sell was executed after the land had already vested in the State under land reform laws
2. `vendor_title_defective` is TRUE — the seller had no valid title at the time of executing the sale agreement
3. `sale_deed_based_on_gpa` is TRUE — the registered sale deed is derived from a GPA or unregistered agreement
4. `specific_performance_suit_dismissed` is TRUE — the vendor’s suit for specific performance was dismissed for default
5. `revalidation_attempted` is TRUE — an attempt was made to validate an old unregistered agreement years later without registration

## CONDITION: WHEN THIS JUDGMENT DOES NOT APPLY

1. When the sale is based on a duly registered sale deed with a valid chain of title and the vendor had lawful ownership — in such cases, Suraj Lamp applies only to the extent of rebuttable presumption, not outright invalidity.

2. When the dispute arises under SARFAESI Act enforcement and the borrower challenges possession — this judgment does not apply to SARFAESI proceedings where the secured asset is validly mortgaged and enforcement is procedural; in such cases, Kanaiyalal or Celir LLP would apply.

3. When the land was not vested in the State and the vendor had lawful possession — if the title is undisputed and only possession is in question, traditional civil principles of adverse possession or de facto possession may apply.

## STATUTORY CONTEXT

Primary law: Andhra Pradesh Land Reforms (Ceiling on Agricultural Holdings) Act, 1973  
Primary provision: Vesting of surplus land under Section 10(1) — "On the date specified in the notification, the surplus land shall vest in the State Government free from all encumbrances."  
Instrument level: OTHER  
Nature of provision: MANDATORY — once notification is issued, vesting is automatic and extinguishes private ownership.

Secondary law: Registration Act, 1908  
Provision: Section 17 — "Any document which purports to create, declare, assign, limit or extinguish any right, title or interest in immovable property of value Rs.100 and upwards, shall be registered."  
Section 49 — "No document required to be registered shall affect any immovable property unless it has been registered."  
Nature: MANDATORY — non-registration renders the document inadmissible in evidence and ineffective to transfer title.

## RELATIONSHIP TO OTHER JUDGMENTS

Affirmed: Suraj Lamp & Industries Pvt. Ltd. v. State of Haryana & Anr. (2012) 1 SCC 656  
  Reiterated that SA/GPA/will transactions do not convey title and cannot be recognised as valid modes of transfer of immovable property. Such transactions are not protected under Section 53-A unless supported by a valid registered deed.

Distinguishes: Balkrishna Dattatraya Galande v. Balkrishna Rambharose Gupta (2020) 19 SCC 119  
  While both cases require proof of actual possession, Galande dealt with tenant-landlord disputes in civil courts with evidence. Here, in a writ petition under Article 226, the burden is higher and mere documents are insufficient without proof of physical possession.

Follows: S.P. Chengalvaraya Naidu (D) by LRs v. Jagannath (D) by LRs and Ors. (1994) 1 SCC 1  
  Emphasised that fraud vitiates title, and courts must look beyond documents when there is a cloud on title due to suspicious transactions.
